## The scorecard: the audit's ten areas

The audit scored the site 4.4 of 10 on October 16 (the answer 3, big movers 2, truth machinery 5, information architecture and naming 4, design and feel 6, speed and weight 8, search and discovery 4, trust 5, daily rhythm 5, app readiness 2). Those ten areas are this board's scorecard, scored as the audit's section 1 defines a 9 for each, by the reviewer after every merge and by the strategist after the program; the audit's own lift estimates per sprint (the "Lifts" column) are the targets each sprint is judged against. Nine out of ten means a mean of 9.0 across the ten areas, and the audit itself says the build buys about 8.0 by the end of 9.8 with the last point earned over weeks by the verdict record, the rhythm and the app; the board's job is the 8.0, measured honestly, and the strategist's review after 9.8 says what the remaining point needs. Two lines are never allowed to fall: truth machinery and daily rhythm never score lower after a merge than before it.

Standing laws the reviewer fails any sprint on, whatever the row says: a figure without a source; the same fact printed twice; two windows of one fact without their labels; "because" anywhere but a Confirmed verdict, "Likely" only where the timing test and the mechanics agree (ruling 1 of October 16); any new model spend beyond the $1.50 a day cap (ruling 2); any AI disclosure on the site and any personal name for the editor, who is "Crypto Cronkite" (ruling 4, which overrules the audit's decision 4 on disclosure); an em dash; a stale figure shown as live; a URL changed; a canary name check not seen red on the old name first (U-9).
