# Program 5: the five-minute read, GoCheckMyCrypto, October 5, 2026

Written by the strategy desk from Jack's direction of October 4 and 5 and the design canvas "GoCheckMyCrypto Redesign" (six artboards: home at phone and desktop, the Bitcoin coin page, the Top 100, navigation, the tile). The canvas is Jack's reference; this document is the build's. Where the two differ, this document governs and the canvas is corrected.

## 1. The brief, in Jack's words and the desk's

The site wins on one promise: a reader opens it and knows what is going on in crypto in under five minutes, with no bloat, no ads, nothing in the way, and the option to go deeper. Every question a reader brings in those five minutes is answered on the first screen, in order, from data the site already has or can take for free, presented so well that the data itself is the product. We do not write our way to this; we curate, rank, check and present. Writing stays where it earns its keep: the Brief once a day, and the Record a few times a week. The look rises to the standard of GoCheckMyWeather and Vital: data that looks like it belongs in a game, on paper and red, with nothing decorative. The site stays ready to become an iPhone app the way GoCheckMyWeather did, so nothing built here depends on a mouse.

The laws of the family hold: no em dashes anywhere, US spelling, no AI mention on the site, nothing fabricated, every figure stamped with its zone, the copy law of September 16 (less is more, the details live in the data and the stories, chrome never explains itself), the September 12 rule that only Jack changes how the newsroom executes and publishes, and the standing rules U-1 to U-16: U-1 to U-13 in the Sports handoff, U-14 to U-16 in the Weather handoff (docs/HANDOFF.md in gocheckmyweather), copied into the Crypto handoff at Sprint 1a's close.

## 2. The seven questions, and the order of the page

In the first five minutes a reader wants, in this order: is Bitcoin up or down, by how much, since yesterday and inside the week's range; what narrative is driving it; did anything happen that explains it; is money coming in or leaving; is the market stretched or scared; what else moved, in the top 20 first and the top 100 only for the standouts; what is on today's calendar; and, for a holder, did my coins move. The home page answers them top to bottom, in that order, and nothing else sits above the fold. The Board in full, the whale chart, the Brief and the Record follow below.

## 3. The home page

Phone first, 390 wide, the desktop a two-column arrangement of the same blocks (left: the tape, the narrative, since yesterday, the why, the wire; right: the money, the mood, movers, today, mine), then full-width rows for the Board, the whale chart, the Brief and the Record. The artboards Main.dc.html and HomeDesktop.dc.html on the canvas are the picture.

The tape. Bitcoin large in the mono with its 24-hour change, a seven-day line drawn to scale from the week's closes with the low and high printed under it, ETH, SOL and XRP small beside it (the desktop adds the total cap), the live stamp with the word "live" and its time and zone. The tape is the only live surface on the site, as the data contract made it; its sparkline draws once on load and never loops. Under it one line: everything below the tape is the Board, as of the snapshot's stamp.

The narrative. A dark band with one sentence of two clauses in the serif: which way the tape is drifting and on what, and what kind of day the news is. It is written by the Edition run once a day from the Board's readings and the wire's top three, and it is deterministic between runs: when the Board's readings cross a threshold during the day (a sign change on ETF net, Fear & Greed crossing a band, funding leaving calm) the build rewrites the sentence from a fixed table of clauses, never from a model, and the band's small line names the readings it was written from. It never predicts; it describes.

Since yesterday. Three lines in the serif with the delta in the mono: Bitcoin against its 200-day average, the whole market with dominance, and Fear & Greed with yesterday's figure. The Board already computes all three.

The why. The day's checked story: the badge, the headline, two lines of what was checked and against what, and the "reads with" line naming the Board reading it touches. Under the newsroom change in section 9 this is the top wire item's checked note rather than a written story.

Moving the market. The day's top five clusters from the wire, in the desk's own one-line words, numbered, each with the Board reading it touches, the source count, the primary-source mark where one is present, and the link to the source; the one the desk checked carries its badge and the rest carry the wire mark. One line under the list says what a wire line is and is not.

The money. Three tiles: spot ETF net with its day, stablecoin float with its since-yesterday, whale net with its window and "all coins."

The mood. Three tiles: funding per 8 hours with a word for it (calm, warm, hot, from fixed thresholds printed on the Learn page), open interest change with the venue figure, Fear & Greed with the word and yesterday's number.

Moving today, top 20. The three largest rises and the largest fall among the top 20 by market cap, each with its rank; then one line for standouts in the top 100 moving more than 5 percent either way, or "none today."

Today. The calendar line: the day's scheduled events from calendar.json (section 8), each with its time in ET and its source, or "nothing scheduled" when the day has none.

Mine. The reader's pinned coins from the device-only watchlist with price and 24-hour change; when none are pinned, one line that says pins come from any coin page.

Below the fold. The Board as eight tiles on the one tile pattern (section 7); the whale chart, thirteen weeks of bars off and onto exchanges with the sign convention under it; the Brief with its stamp; the Record's lanes with one piece each.

## 4. The coin page

Coin.dc.html is the picture. The price large with today and the week, the live stamp; timeframes 1D, 7D, 30D, 90D, 1Y with 1D new, a volume pane under the line and a 50-day and 200-day toggle, drawn from the same source the Top 100 uses; a "what applies to this coin" grid, for Bitcoin: RSI 14, the 50 over 200 state, the drawdown from the 12-month high, spot ETF net, funding, fees, and for other coins the readings that exist for them and nothing invented; the Chart Master's read as a dark card with its date in its headline and a line saying when the figures above are newer; checked stories about this coin with their badges and dates; the source line; Pin. The page is the chart product the audit asked for; the Chart Master page becomes the dated read and the live boards, nothing more.

## 5. The Top 100

Top100.dc.html is the picture. Search by symbol or name, the sort state visible on its button, the columns rank, coin with name and cap, price, 24-hour change, and the seven-day line drawn to scale from the week's closes; stablecoins shown and marked; the rank rule said once above the table in the words on the artboard, so a skipped number reads as the screen and not a bug; the stamp with its zone; ten rows at open and "Show 11 to 100" below. The audit's "updated 12:08" never appears again.

## 6. Navigation

Nav.dc.html is the picture. Task names with the brand names small under them: Markets (The Board), Flows (Whale Watch), Charts (Chart Master), News (Checked), Brief (The Edition), Record (Worth keeping), Learn (The Board explained), and search in the header row on desktop. The phone bar carries five: Markets, Flows, Charts, News, Brief; Record and Learn sit behind search and in the footer. Search covers coins, stories and the Record from a static index built at publish, no third-party service. Every control is a real link or button with a name, 44 pixels tall on a phone, and nothing depends on hover.

## 7. The tile and the visual system

Tile.dc.html is the picture, and its five rules govern every tile on every page: the label in plain words; the figure in the mono, colored only when direction means something; the window written on the tile; the since-yesterday line in words; the source and the stamp with its zone on the tile with "what this means" opening the Learn page for that reading. No icons, no gradients, no persona art, no explainer paragraph under a tile.

Tokens: the existing paper #FBFAF6, ink #15171C, rule red #B42318, link #123A5E, up green #12803B, verified green #12603B, review amber #7A5200, line #E3E0D6, white cards; the muted gray darkened to #555A63 for contrast; the dark band #15171C with paper text. Type: Newsreader for headlines and the narrative, IBM Plex Mono for every figure and stamp, Inter for labels and body. Dark mode kept and designed, not inverted. Motion, in the Weather manner: the tape's line draws once, a live price change fades its digits, tiles never animate, nothing loops, and prefers-reduced-motion stops all of it. Charts draw only published points, straight segments, no smoothing, the rule wx-chart keeps on Weather.

Performance is a feature of the design, not a hope: the home page at 375 on the site's own host under 300 KB before any interaction, with the fonts self-hosted and subset; no third-party script of any kind; the only runtime requests are the snapshot and the tape's live read; largest contentful paint under 2.5 seconds on a phone on production, measured and printed in every report, against the 4,540 ms the handoff recorded in September.

## 8. The data assets, each with its source, cadence and cost

Everything here is public and read by the build or the Worker; all of it is keyless except FRED, whose free key lives in the repository's secrets like the others and never in a file. Nothing is invented where a source is missing: the slot prints a placeholder sentence that names what it is waiting for.

- Prices, caps, 24-hour and 7-day changes, the seven-day lines: the one endpoint the contract named, with its sparkline field, so the tape's line and the Top 100's lines cost no second request.
- Dominance, total cap, stablecoin float: the global endpoint already read.
- ETF flows and the issuer table: the source the Record's living table already uses.
- Whale flows: the public archive already read, with its window printed.
- Funding and open interest: the venue data already read; the mood words come from fixed thresholds on the Learn page.
- Fear & Greed: alternative.me, once a day, snapshot only, never on the tape.
- Fees and the next difficulty adjustment: mempool.space.
- Movers: computed from the same price read: largest rises and fall in the top 20, standouts over 5 percent in the top 100.
- The wire (wire.json): the editor's ranked clusters for the day, each with the desk's one-line version from the cluster's key fact, the source count, a primary-source flag, the links, and the Board reading it touches from the tag rules; written by the Edition run with the model's ranking once a day, and refreshed between runs on the Worker's ticks by the deterministic desk editor, which re-counts sources and re-orders without a model call. A wire line is never an outlet's headline verbatim.
- The checked note: for the top wire item that clears the verifier, two sentences, what the primary source says and what could not be confirmed, with the badge; the verifier's cost and nothing more.
- The narrative line: the Edition writes it once a day from the Board and the wire's top three; between runs the fixed clause table rewrites it on threshold crossings.
- The calendar (calendar.json), built from five sources, each named on its line. Macro release dates (CPI, PCE, the jobs report, GDP, retail sales) from the FRED releases/dates endpoint with include_release_dates_with_no_data set, which returns the official future release calendar; FRED keys are free and the key lives in the repository's secrets like the others, never in a file. FOMC meeting dates from the Federal Reserve's published schedule, kept as a yearly file in the repository with the source page named, since the Fed publishes no API for it; US market holidays the same way. The next Bitcoin and Ether options expiries with their open interest from Deribit's public endpoints, which need no key; the desk reads them live before building and prints the fields it found. The next difficulty adjustment from mempool.space. ETF decision deadlines computed from the Federal Register's keyless documents API: every notice of a proposed rule change to list or amend a crypto exchange-traded product, and every notice of designation of a longer period, is fetched by search term, the designated date is read from the notice's own text where it states one, and the statutory clocks (45 days from publication, extendable to 90, then 180 and 240 after proceedings) are computed where it does not; each deadline line links the notice. Token unlocks have no free source of record, DefiLlama's unlocks sit behind its $300-a-month API tier and the rest are paid or scraped, so the first build carries a hand-kept vesting file for the top 20 by market cap from each project's own published schedule, refreshed monthly by the desk with the source named, and a dated line Jack can add by hand. Community event calendars with free keys may feed leads to the wire, never the calendar. The today line shows the day's entries with times in ET and a source on each.
- Pins: the device-only watchlist that exists, shown on the home page.

Cost: the wire, the movers, the narrative's threshold rewrites and the calendar add no model calls; the checked note replaces the daily story's writer stage, which is the largest model spend in the run. The run's cost is printed in every close as it is now.

## 9. The newsroom's half, Jack's ruling

Jack's words, October 5: worry less about writing and more about putting the best relevant stories at the top based on what the desk thinks is relevant and accurate; the desk vets, we publish, we do not rewrite, we show the source; take the information and use it as an asset, not a cost. Under the September 12 rule this is Jack's change to how the newsroom publishes, and it reads as follows. The daily written story ends. The wire is the news surface: the day's ranked clusters in the desk's one-line words with sources counted and linked. The top wire item that clears the verifier carries a checked note of two sentences and the badge; the badges stay the three of October 4. The Brief continues once a day as the Board explained, with the narrative line as its lead. The Record continues as the pieces worth keeping in its four lanes, a few a week, written in full, since written pages are what search indexes. The caged breaking runs continue as they are. The cadence line on the News page changes to say this: the day's stories ranked and sourced by the desk, one checked, the Brief every evening.

## 10. App readiness

Nothing here builds an app. Everything here keeps the site ready for one: the phone bar and pages work full screen in standalone display, a web app manifest with the site's icon, 44-pixel targets, no hover-only control, the same in-app marker contract GoCheckMyWeather uses so a WKWebView shell with a native tab bar can carry the site later without a rebuild. When Jack says the word, the Weather app's shell is the model and its build desk has done it once.
