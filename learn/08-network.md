# Network fees and difficulty: what a quiet or busy chain tells you

*Every other tile on the Board measures money. The network tile measures activity: how crowded the Bitcoin network is right now, and how much computing power is protecting it. Both say something about the market that price cannot.*

[LIVE FROM THE BOARD: fee rate, fee band, difficulty and next adjustment, as-of stamp, source. Link: See it on the Board.]

## Fees, and the unit on the tile

Bitcoin transactions wait in a queue called the mempool until a miner includes them in a block. Space in each block is limited, so when many people want in at once, they bid for it. The bid is the fee, measured in satoshis per virtual byte, written sat/vB. A satoshi is one hundred-millionth of a bitcoin; a virtual byte is a unit of the transaction's size. The fee rate is the price of space.

A low number, in the single digits, means the queue is short and the chain is quiet. A high number, in the hundreds, means the queue is long and people are paying up to jump it.

## What fee spikes tell you

Fees rise when a lot of people move coins at the same time. That happens for two very different reasons, and the tile cannot tell them apart on its own, which is why it sits beside the whale tile.

The first reason is fear or excitement about price. In a crash, people rush coins to exchanges to sell; in a mania, they rush to buy and to move purchases into storage. Either way the chain gets crowded.

The second reason has nothing to do with price. Waves of activity unrelated to trading, such as periods when people used the chain to record data or trade collectibles, have pushed fees up while price did nothing.

The useful read is the pairing. Fees spiking while whale deposits surge is a rush to sell. Fees spiking while coins leave exchanges is a rush to hold. Fees flat during a big rally means the action is happening on exchanges and in the ETFs, not on the chain itself, which has been the normal pattern since the spot funds launched.

## Difficulty, and what it says about miners

Miners compete to add blocks by solving a computational puzzle. The network adjusts the difficulty of that puzzle every 2,016 blocks, about every two weeks, so that a block is found roughly every ten minutes regardless of how many miners are competing.

Rising difficulty means more computing power joined the network, which means miners are investing in equipment and electricity. They do that when they expect mining to stay profitable. Falling difficulty means miners are switching off, which tends to happen after price falls or after a halving cuts their revenue.

Difficulty is slow and steady by design. It will not tell you what price does next week. It tells you whether the people with the largest fixed costs in the system are expanding or retreating.

## What it does not tell you

Fees measure activity, not direction. A busy chain can be busy with buyers or sellers. Difficulty measures the miners' commitment, not the market's. Read the tile for context on the other numbers, not as a signal on its own.

## Where the data comes from

Fee rates and mempool depth come from mempool.space, a public view of the Bitcoin network, read at every site build. Difficulty and the estimated next adjustment come from the same source.
