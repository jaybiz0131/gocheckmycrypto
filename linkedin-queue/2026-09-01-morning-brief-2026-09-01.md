Cronos validators halted their chain and rolled back 10,961 blocks to undo a $74-75 million exploit of the Tectonic lending protocol. The price-manipulation attack forced an on-chain intervention that recovered most funds but left roughly $6.3 million already moved to Ethereum out of reach. The incident tested whether a blockchain network can unwind a major loss through consensus, a step that sits uneasily between operational recovery and the immutability that defines the medium.

Chain halts and rollbacks raise hard questions about what decentralization means when the alternative is catastrophic loss. The broader market held steady on ETF flows and fresh corporate bitcoin purchases, but the Cronos event underscores the operational risk that still sits inside DeFi protocols and the chains that host them.
